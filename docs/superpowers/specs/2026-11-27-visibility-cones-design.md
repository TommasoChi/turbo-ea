# Visibility cones

Design for owner review. Baseline: `main` at `1ae9ae9e5`, including Cost Controller (PR #94). A visibility cone is the set of card grants a manager receives from stakeholder assignments held by people in subordinate Organization positions.

## Why

A manager needs to work on the same cards as the people they manage. Their own manager needs the same reach through every level beneath them. Today these grants have to be assigned card by card. A manager can miss a card even when their reporting line is already recorded in Organization.

## Goals

- Derive each manager's card grants from the Organization Parent tree and current stakeholder assignments.
- Apply the grants to reads and the writes those stakeholder roles allow. Keep existing application and function gates.
- Explain inherited grants in the card UI. Keep personal views and actions tied to direct assignments.
- Ship three independently verifiable changes. Enable the cone only with consistent card and list authorization.

## Non-goals

- Inheriting another person's application role, application permission keys, type or subtype cells, or access to an application function.
- Reporting lines between other card types, temporary or per-user delegation, or new tables for people, positions or grants.
- Fixing the broader hidden-card write response, bulk-edit authorization or who can assign data-bearing roles on ordinary cards. Those concerns were raised in the brief and need their own changes. Position grants and Organization reparenting are in scope.

## The rule

A position is an Organization card. A position holder has a direct assignment to a role on that card whose definition is marked as a position role. The seeded Responsible is the default position role. Observer cannot confer a position, even when the user manually follows an Organization card.

For a user U, start at every Organization position U holds. Follow `parent_id` down through Organization children only. Collect the distinct position holders of all strict descendants. A vacant position contributes no holder. Traversal continues through it. Multiple holders at a position all count. A person with several positions uses the union of their subtrees. A person reached more than once contributes each direct assignment once. Exclude U from the subordinate set. Peers on the same position or on sibling positions gain no grant from that relationship. Traversal follows positions, even when their holders appear elsewhere in the tree.

For a target card C of any type, the effective stakeholder grants of U come from U's direct roles on C and the direct roles on C of the subordinates just found. The rule also applies when C is an Organization card. Resolve each role by C's type and use its current active definition. A role that is undefined or archived grants nothing. Direct assignments retain their current authority. No inherited assignment row is created.

The effective permission is still the union of the caller's own application grant and qualifying card grants. The caller must satisfy independent application function requirements. A subordinate's powerful application role contributes zero grants. Inherited `card.view_costs` and `card.edit_costs` are card grants subject to the shipped cost rules. The card and its costs must be visible before a cost edit. A Cost Controller assignment can give a manager cost access on the target card while leaving general editing unavailable. PPM still requires the manager's own function permissions. Licensing keeps its existing conditions with the inherited card grants as inputs. The treatment of inherited grants under a View deny and the treatment of inherited `card.manage_stakeholders` are owner decisions below.

Example: the director holds IT Direction. The manager holds its child IT Governance. You and your colleague hold separate children of IT Governance. Assume each person has a direct read-and-edit stakeholder role on their own example card, no other relevant grant, and no applicable deny. “Read and edit” means the card's general data.

| Reader | Your card | Colleague's card | Manager's card | Director's card |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| You | Read and edit | — | — | — |
| Colleague | — | Read and edit | — | — |
| Manager | Read and edit | Read and edit | Read and edit | — |
| Director | Read and edit | Read and edit | Read and edit | Read and edit |

## Requirements

- **R1. Position role data.** Add one boolean to `stakeholder_role_definitions` with a sequential Alembic migration. Expose it in the Organization type's Stakeholders panel. Fresh seeds mark the built-in Responsible by default. An admin's saved setting survives subsequent seeds. Users cannot turn Observer or a non-Organization role into a position role. The existing-install activation policy is an owner decision. **Check:** schema upgrade and restart tests cover seed, saved false and invalid role/type combinations.
- **R2. One stakeholder grant resolver.** Use a single grant definition for one card, sets of cards and SQL predicates. Phase 1 returns exactly today's direct grants. Route the four authorization entry points listed above through it as their phases dictate. Retain the assignment's source for authorized explanations. **Check:** existing grants, denies, invalid role definitions and cost/license gates have the same results before activation.
- **R3. Position traversal.** Use a recursive SQLAlchemy query following Organization Parent edges with explicit cycle protection. The owner decides whether to support every valid existing depth or introduce a product limit. If a limit is introduced, validate chart depth before activation and enforce it on future chart writes. Partial traversal must never silently drop grants. Reject or safely terminate malformed or cyclic input without widening access. Stop at non-Organization edges. Resolve the position graph and the definitions used as grant inputs from current data. Reuse results within a request where appropriate. An assignment change, a role flag change or a Parent change takes effect for subsequent checks after commit. A worker must not continue using a revoked inherited grant until an existing five-minute permission cache expires. **Check:** cover deep reports, vacant intermediate positions, peers, multiple holders and positions, reparenting, removal, duplicate paths, malformed hierarchy and post-commit revocation from a separate worker.
- **R4. Protect the organization chart.** Treat grant-producing changes as authorization changes. The approved gate must cover role flag toggles, assignment/replacement/removal of position holders, and placement or movement of positions. Classify equivalent create, delete/archive, write, bulk, import, rollback and integration paths before enabling the cone. Preserve the existing hierarchy cycle guard and enforce any new Organization depth policy the owner selects. The chosen gate must be enforced on the server and must not be granted by an inherited card permission alone. **Check:** an ordinary Responsible cannot change the cone through a disallowed entry point. An authorized move revokes the old manager's derived grant and grants the new manager the appropriate access after commit.
- **R5. - **R6. Row visibility.** In phase 3 use a relational subquery for inherited visibility in list and reference queries. Avoid loading thousands of inherited card IDs per request. Keep `clause() == None` on the existing unrestricted paths when the caller already has full visibility for that mode. Preserve explicit `include_stakeholders=False` as application-only. **Check:** shared parity fixtures, a comparison of two subtree sizes at a fixed page and candidate size, with query count and parameters independent of the number of inherited card IDs, and no extra cone query on the unrestricted fast path.
- **R7. Personal views and assignment identity.** Keep My Cards, direct stakeholder lists and counts, notifications, personal event feeds, and `me/observe` based on direct rows. Continue filtering the returned cards with the existing read scope as appropriate. Keep editing, removing, assigning and deduplicating real assignments separate from displaying effective grants. **Check:** a manager can read an inherited card through ordinary inventory after phase 3, while that card still cannot enter My Cards or start a subscription through inheritance alone.
- **R8. Effective permissions and UI.** With the cone enabled, the card UI follows server-calculated permissions. Display inherited roles separately and read-only in Stakeholders. ## Agent's calls (check them)

The confirmed hierarchy uses Organization's Parent field. The choices below are open until the owner selects them.

1. **View deny.** Allow a qualifying inherited `card.view` to recover the card, as a direct stakeholder grant does today, or make a matching type/subtype View deny block inherited grants while keeping direct grants intact. Under the restrictive option the deny would suppress every inherited grant on the card, including a domain write grant when the manager can still read through a direct assignment. **Recommend parity with direct grants.** It gives one per-card grant rule and requires strong protections around the chart. The stricter option preserves explicit walls for newly derived access and requires a separate branch in every read and write decision.
2. **Inherited `card.manage_stakeholders`.** Inherit it and adopt an approved rule for assigning roles that carry data access, or exclude it from inheritance while preserving direct and application authority. **Recommend excluding it initially.** The shipped Cost Controller change removed the application grant from Member and BPM Admin. Direct stakeholder or custom application grants can still authorize assignment of a Cost Controller on an ordinary card. Inheriting this key would extend that assignment authority to managers and makes the broader role-assignment policy a release dependency.
3. **Who manages the chart.** Use `admin.metamodel` to change position-role flags, require application `stakeholders.manage` to change position holders, and require a dedicated application permission to place or move positions. The alternative reserves all three operations to administrators. **Recommend the separate application gates.** They allow separate owners for role definitions, assignments and the chart. Admin-only reduces delegation and has a smaller policy surface. Ordinary or inherited `card.edit` on an Organization must not become chart authority by accident. Decide whether chart editors also need ordinary card edit authority and whether authorized workspace apply is a separate administrative path.
4. **Creator assignment on Organization.** A creator without authority to appoint a holder can receive an approved unmarked role or the request to appoint a holder can be rejected. **Recommend keeping creation available with an unmarked role if the separate design defines one.** Rejecting the appointment preserves the gate when no such fallback exists. Decide the fallback and how the chosen behavior applies to a create request that also sets Parent.
5. **Activation for existing workspaces.** Backfill Responsible immediately and enable the cone at the phase 3 upgrade, or seed Responsible for new sites and require explicit activation for existing charts. **Recommend explicit activation for existing charts.** Existing Organization data may map teams and contacts without encoding reporting authority. Automatic activation gives the requested behavior sooner and makes the migration itself a grant change. Recommend a separate workspace activation step, with imported flags preserving the target's activation state. In an inactive workspace, phase 2 protects configuration of position flags and preserves existing chart operations that confer no derived access. Switching on inheritance requires the approved gates to be in place. The alternative uses a marked role as implicit activation once phase 3 is released. That makes a true flag in an import sufficient to activate grants. A preflight can show the resulting scope change.
6. **Inactive positions and holders.** Count existing archived positions and deactivated holders, or exclude them from grant calculation. **Recommend excluding deactivated holders and archived position branches.** This prevents stale charts and accounts from granting power. A valid vacant position still allows traversal. Under the recommended policy, traversal stops at an archived node. An active holder below it can still start from their own active position. The owner can instead choose to suppress the whole branch, including those starting positions, or to traverse archived positions and retain reach to live descendants. These choices change which grants archiving revokes.
7. **Write provenance.** Preserve the initiating actor in the existing audit trail and add per-write inherited provenance, or defer the extra source metadata. **Recommend deferring the extra fields.** They require a stable explanation when several grants qualify and extra filtering of historical sources. The existing audit of changes to the chart is required either way.
8. **Maximum depth.** Preserve all valid existing Organization trees with cycle-safe traversal, or introduce a product limit and reject activation for trees that exceed it. **Recommend preserving existing depths.** A product limit gives a predictable traversal ceiling and needs an explicit maximum and a remediation path for existing workspaces.
9. **Writes to unreadable cards.** Require readability before any inherited write, or keep each endpoint's existing visibility rule for inherited writes too. **Recommend requiring readability for inherited writes.** This keeps the new grant from authorizing a blind write. Direct and application grants keep their existing semantics. Test a custom role with `card.edit=true` and no card-view grant.

Authorization checks use the effective resolver unless they intentionally require application-only authority. Identity, duplicate checks and reports of actual assignments stay direct. For the BPM draft actions, `ProcessFlowTab` uses `can_edit_draft`, computed by the workflow endpoint with `_can_edit_draft`. Test those actual controls and actions together. The existing `can_bpm_edit` field has a different predicate and no production UI read was found (`frontend/src/features/bpm/ProcessFlowTab.tsx:901`, `backend/app/api/v1/bpm_workflow.py:105`, `:1386`, `backend/app/services/permission_service.py:542`).

## Acceptance

- The example yields the four stated rows of read and edit rights. A subordinate with a powerful application role contributes zero privileges without relevant stakeholder assignments.
- The cone works for direct reports, deeper descendants, multiple positions and multiple holders. Peers and ancestors never contribute through those relationships. Changing the chart or removing a grant takes effect after commit.
- Users without direct or derived access receive the same hidden-card behavior as today. No hidden card appears in a list, count, reference, nested payload or source explanation. The extended parity tests and sentinel sweep prove denial and positive access.
- A manager with an inherited Cost Controller role can manage costs without general card edit when the card, its costs and the relevant function are available. General card editing and cost visibility still require a separate cost-edit grant to write costs. License permissions, personal views and `me/observe` keep their existing rules. The selected policies for View deny, chart management and stakeholder assignment are asserted on the API.
- The migration and workspace transfer preserve the selected rollout policy. Old bundles give no positive position flag. The UI matches server authorization, the unrestricted fast path stays intact, and phase 3 is the first release that can activate the complete cone.
