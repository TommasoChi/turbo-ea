# Visibility cones

Design for owner review. Baseline: `main` at `77881d477`. A visibility cone is the set of card grants a manager receives from stakeholder assignments held by people in subordinate Organization positions.

## Why

A manager needs to work on the same cards as the people they manage. Their own manager needs the same reach through every level beneath them. Today these grants have to be assigned card by card. A manager can miss a card even when their reporting line is already recorded in Organization.

## Goals

- Derive each manager's card grants from the Organization Parent tree and current stakeholder assignments.
- Apply the grants to reads and the writes those stakeholder roles allow. Keep existing application and function gates.
- Explain inherited grants in the card UI. Keep personal views and actions tied to direct assignments.
- Ship three independently verifiable changes. Enable the cone only with consistent card and list authorization.

## Non-goals

- Inheriting another person's application role, application permission keys, type or subtype cells, or access to an application function.
- Reporting lines between other card types, temporary or per-user delegation, or new tables for people, positions or grants.
- Fixing the broader hidden-card write response, bulk-edit authorization or who can assign data-bearing roles on ordinary cards. Those concerns were raised in the brief and need their own changes. Position grants and Organization reparenting are in scope.

## The rule

A position is an Organization card. A position holder has a direct assignment to a role on that card whose definition is marked as a position role. The seeded Responsible is the default position role. Observer cannot confer a position, even when the user manually follows an Organization card.

For a user U, start at every Organization position U holds. Follow `parent_id` down through Organization children only. Collect the distinct position holders of all strict descendants. A vacant position contributes no holder. Traversal continues through it. Multiple holders at a position all count. A person with several positions uses the union of their subtrees. A person reached more than once contributes each direct assignment once. Exclude U from the subordinate set. Peers on the same position or on sibling positions gain no grant from that relationship. Traversal follows positions, even when their holders appear elsewhere in the tree.

For a target card C of any type, the effective stakeholder grants of U come from U's direct roles on C and the direct roles on C of the subordinates just found. The rule also applies when C is an Organization card. Resolve each role by C's type and use its current active definition. A role that is undefined or archived grants nothing. Direct assignments retain their current authority. No inherited assignment row is created.

The effective permission is still the union of the caller's own application grant and qualifying card grants. The caller must satisfy independent application function requirements. A subordinate's powerful application role contributes zero grants. Inherited `card.view_costs` and, when defined by the Cost Controller work, `card.edit_costs` apply to that card and remain subject to the existing view and write prerequisites. A read grant cannot open a cost edit. Licensing follows its existing rules with the inherited stakeholder inputs. The treatment of inherited grants under a View deny and the treatment of inherited `card.manage_stakeholders` are owner decisions below.

Example: the director holds IT Direction. The manager holds its child IT Governance. You and your colleague hold separate children of IT Governance. Assume each person has a direct read-and-edit stakeholder role on their own example card, no other relevant grant, and no applicable deny. “Read and edit” means the card's general data.

| Reader | Your card | Colleague's card | Manager's card | Director's card |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| You | Read and edit | — | — | — |
| Colleague | — | Read and edit | — | — |
| Manager | Read and edit | Read and edit | Read and edit | — |
| Director | Read and edit | Read and edit | Read and edit | Read and edit |

## Requirements

- **R1. Position role data.** Add one boolean to `stakeholder_role_definitions` with a sequential Alembic migration. Expose it in the Organization type's Stakeholders panel. Fresh seeds mark the built-in Responsible by default. An admin's saved setting survives subsequent seeds. Users cannot turn Observer or a non-Organization role into a position role. The existing-install activation policy is an owner decision. **Check:** schema upgrade and restart tests cover seed, saved false and invalid role/type combinations.
- **R2. One stakeholder grant resolver.** Use a single grant definition for one card, sets of cards and SQL predicates. Phase 1 returns exactly today's direct grants. Route the four authorization entry points listed above through it as their phases dictate. Retain the assignment's source for authorized explanations. **Check:** existing grants, denies, invalid role definitions and cost/license gates have the same results before activation.
- **R3. Position traversal.** Use a depth-bounded recursive SQLAlchemy query following Organization Parent edges. Reject or safely terminate malformed or cyclic input without widening access. Stop at non-Organization edges. Resolve position holders from authoritative assignments in the current transaction. Use request-local reuse where appropriate. An assignment change, a role flag change or a Parent change takes effect for subsequent checks after commit. A later cache must preserve revocation across workers. **Check:** cover deep reports, vacant intermediate positions, peers, multiple holders and positions, reparenting, removal, duplicate paths and malformed hierarchy.
- **R4. Protect the organization chart.** Treat grant-producing changes as authorization changes. The approved gate must cover role flag toggles, assignment/replacement/removal of position holders, and placement or movement of positions. Classify equivalent create, delete/archive, write, bulk, import, rollback and integration paths before enabling the cone. Preserve cycle and depth guards. The chosen gate must be enforced on the server and must not be granted by an inherited card permission alone. **Check:** an ordinary Responsible cannot change the cone through a disallowed entry point. An authorized move revokes the old manager's derived grant and grants the new manager the appropriate access after commit.
- **R5. Card and domain authorization.** Apply the same inherited grant rule to read, edit and other qualifying card permissions, to bulk cost/license access, and to effective UI permissions. Preserve caller-side application gates and the existing prerequisites for data columns. Treat `card.edit_costs` as a release dependency of the Cost Controller contract. Apply the selected View-deny and stakeholder-management policies consistently. **Check:** cover direct-only users, inherited-only managers, users who have both, callers lacking the required function, and subordinates with powerful application roles but no relevant stakeholder assignment.
- **R6. Row visibility.** In phase 3 use a relational subquery for inherited visibility in list and reference queries. Avoid loading thousands of inherited card IDs per request. Make `clause`, `ref_clause`, `readable`, bulk resolution and single-card checks agree in `inventory` and `module` modes. A Python check that has insufficient evidence must use the same grant definition through a scoped lookup or bounded prefetch. Keep `clause() == None` on the existing unrestricted paths when the caller already has full visibility for that mode. **Check:** shared parity fixtures, a large-subtree check for bounded query count and parameters, and no extra cone query on the unrestricted fast path.
- **R7. Personal views and assignment identity.** Keep My Cards, direct stakeholder lists and counts, notifications, personal event feeds, and `me/observe` based on direct rows. Continue filtering the returned cards with the existing read scope as appropriate. Keep editing, removing, assigning and deduplicating real assignments separate from displaying effective grants. **Check:** a manager can read an inherited card through ordinary inventory after phase 3, while that card still cannot enter My Cards or start a subscription through inheritance alone.
- **R8. Effective permissions and UI.** With the cone enabled, the card UI follows server-calculated permissions. Display inherited roles separately and read-only in Stakeholders. Indicate the Organization path and source holder if the caller can see them. Use a generic inherited explanation when disclosure would reveal a hidden position or private user details. Preserve the wire meaning of the direct role list and document any added provenance fields. **Check:** direct assignments remain editable by authorized callers, inherited entries have no row actions, buttons reflect actual API rights, and hidden source identifiers never enter the response.
- **R9. Workspace transfer.** Extend the existing `StakeholderRoles` section for the flag. Export the flag and direct source assignments only. Import a legacy row with no flag as false, including when a role matches by its natural key. An explicit false stays false. Validate the same type and role constraints as the API, and apply or preview hierarchy and holder changes under the relevant authorization and import rules. **Check:** round-trip true and false into fresh and populated targets, test a legacy bundle, and test a matched role where the imported value differs.
- **R10. Operational contract.** Write English product strings first. Run `/translate` for the other nine locales during implementation and update the manual for position configuration, direct and inherited grants, and rollout. Regenerate OpenAPI if a route or response contract changes. Record administrative changes to position roles, holders and Parent with the existing audit contract. Inherited-write provenance is a separate decision below. **Check:** review audit output for those changes, UI string parity, strict manual build, and OpenAPI parity where applicable.
- **R11. Regression and security evidence.** Extend `backend/tests/services/test_card_read_scope.py`, `backend/tests/services/test_card_read_scope_guard.py`, `backend/tests/api/test_read_scope_sentinel_sweep.py` and `backend/tests/api/test_cards_type_read_permissions.py` as applicable. Assert row, reference, nested payload and aggregate omission. Include a positive control that sees a sentinel card through a valid cone and a peer who cannot see it. Keep the existing CI and mutation thresholds. **Check:** the parity matrix and sentinel sweep pass with cones enabled and disabled. VAPT and the static security review cover the permission and read changes.

## Agent's calls (check them)

The confirmed hierarchy uses Organization's Parent field. The choices below are open until the owner selects them.

1. **View deny.** Allow a qualifying inherited `card.view` to recover the card, as a direct stakeholder grant does today, or make a matching type/subtype View deny block inherited grants while keeping direct grants intact. **Recommend parity with direct grants.** It gives one per-card grant rule and requires strong protections around the chart. The stricter option preserves explicit walls for newly derived access and requires a separate branch in every read and write decision.
2. **Inherited `card.manage_stakeholders`.** Inherit it and adopt an approved rule for assigning roles that carry data access, or exclude it from inheritance while preserving direct and application authority. **Recommend excluding it initially.** This prevents the cone from becoming an indirect way to grant direct data access. Inheriting it matches the full subordinate role and makes the role-assignment policy a release dependency.
3. **Who manages the chart.** Require application `stakeholders.manage` for changing Organization position holders plus an explicit application permission for moving Organization positions, or reserve both operations to administrators. **Recommend the two explicit application gates.** They allow separate owners for assignments and the chart. Admin-only reduces delegation and has a smaller policy surface. Ordinary or inherited `card.edit` on an Organization must not become chart authority by accident. Decide whether chart editors also need ordinary card edit authority and whether authorized workspace apply is a separate administrative path.
4. **Classify the remaining assignment lookups.** Move every match to the effective resolver, or classify each lookup by what it proves. **Recommend classifying by purpose.** A permission decision inherits. Identity, duplicate detection and reporting of actual assignments stay direct. The proposed mapping is below. Independent classification of new or unread call sites remains required before switching them.
5. **Activation for existing workspaces.** Backfill Responsible immediately and enable the cone at the phase 3 upgrade, or seed Responsible for new sites and require explicit activation for existing charts. **Recommend explicit activation for existing charts.** Existing Organization data may map teams and contacts without encoding reporting authority. Automatic activation gives the requested behavior sooner and makes the migration itself a grant change. A preflight that shows the scope change is appropriate if automatic activation is chosen.
6. **Inactive positions and holders.** Count existing archived positions and deactivated holders, or exclude them from grant calculation. **Recommend excluding deactivated holders and archived position branches.** This prevents stale charts and accounts from granting power. A valid vacant position still allows traversal. Traversing through archived nodes would preserve live reports below a retired place and needs a different revocation rule.
7. **Write provenance.** Preserve the initiating actor in the existing audit trail and add per-write inherited provenance, or defer the extra source metadata. **Recommend deferring the extra fields.** They require a stable explanation when several grants qualify and extra filtering of historical sources. The existing audit of changes to the chart is required either way.

## Acceptance

- The example yields the four stated rows of read and edit rights. A subordinate with a powerful application role contributes zero privileges without relevant stakeholder assignments.
- The cone works for direct reports, deeper descendants, multiple positions and multiple holders. Peers and ancestors never contribute through those relationships. Changing the chart or removing a grant takes effect after commit.
- Users without direct or derived access receive the same hidden-card behavior as today. No hidden card appears in a list, count, reference, nested payload or source explanation. The extended parity tests and sentinel sweep prove denial and positive access.
- A manager can use inherited cost or license rights only when the card and existing domain gates allow it. Personal views and `me/observe` retain direct semantics. The selected policies for View deny, chart management and stakeholder assignment are asserted on the API.
- The migration and workspace transfer preserve the selected rollout policy. Old bundles give no positive position flag. The UI matches server authorization, the unrestricted fast path stays intact, and phase 3 is the first release that can activate the complete cone.
